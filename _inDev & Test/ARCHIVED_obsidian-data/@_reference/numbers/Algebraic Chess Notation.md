# Algebraic Chess Notation

**SAN**, or Standard Algebraic Notation, is used to record matches of the game **Chess**. It represents the pieces & squares of the chessboard through the use of letters & numbers. Noting each move with a letter symbolizing the piece played & the coordinates of the square played: the starting square & the destination square.

### How does it work

The **64** squares of the chessboard are marked via a 2D coordinate system, the digits **1** -> **8** (from left to right) for the columns & the lowercase letters **a** -> **h** (from below to top) for the rows.

> [!example]  
> The square at the bottom left of board is called `a1`, the square at the top right is called `h8`

The **Chess Pieces** themselves are symbolized by a letter
- **K** - King
- **Q** - Queen
- **N** - kNight
- **R** - Rook
- **B** - Bishop
- **P** or **nulled** - Pawn

## Reasons for use

Algebraic notation in chess is a standardized system allowing players, coaches, & arbiters to easily & accurately record chess games. It us used to record important games, to analyze past games, and to communicate tournament results to organizers & chess federations  
This notation is the official version utilized by the International Chess Federation
